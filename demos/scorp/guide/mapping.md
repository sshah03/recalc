# Mapping a trial balance to the 2026 Form 1120-S (firm guide)

Classify each trial balance account into exactly one category. The categories are
lines of the 2026 draft Form 1120-S (page 1 and Schedule K), plus three of our own.

| Category | Use for |
|---|---|
| 1a Gross receipts | Sales and fees from the business's trade or business |
| 2 Cost of goods sold | Materials, inventory purchases, lab fees, subcontracted production |
| 5 Other income | Other trade or business income (not interest or dividends) |
| 7 Officer compensation | Wages paid to officers, when booked to their own account |
| 8 Salaries and wages | Wages paid to employees who aren't officers |
| 9 Repairs | Repairs and maintenance |
| 11 Rents | Rent for business property |
| 12 Taxes and licenses | Payroll taxes, business licenses, property tax |
| 13 Interest | Interest on business debt |
| 14 Depreciation (book) | Book depreciation expense; the return uses the tax figure from the fixed-asset schedule |
| 16 Advertising | Advertising and marketing |
| 17 Pension plans | Employer retirement plan contributions |
| 18 Employee benefits | Health insurance and other benefits for employees |
| 20 Other deductions | Other ordinary and necessary business expenses: insurance, professional fees, software, travel, contractors, fuel, utilities |
| Meals (50%) | Business meals; half is deductible on line 20 and half is nondeductible |
| K4 Interest income | Interest income: separately stated, not on page 1 |
| K12a Charitable | Charitable contributions: separately stated |
| K16c Nondeductible | Penalties and fines, and premiums on officer life insurance where the company is the beneficiary |
| K16d Distributions | Distributions to shareholders, including personal expenses paid for a shareholder |
| Balance sheet | Assets, liabilities, capital stock and retained earnings |
| Needs review | Anything the documents don't settle, or that needs the preparer's judgment |

Read the client's documents (the engagement facts, the 2025 return summary, the W-3,
the fixed-asset schedule, the distributions ledger and the client's email) when an
account's name alone doesn't settle it. A personal expense of a shareholder paid by
the company is not a business deduction.
